When does hardening help? Predicting where shape continuation beats rigid starts

Journal · Families

Abstract

Packing searches usually move rigid pieces. Hardening instead starts every piece as its inscribed disk and morphs it into the target shape while the container compresses. The parent project showed hardening is not a better default than rigid starts, but a different search: on squares in a square it reached packings no other method found, while showing no advantage on triangles, hexagons, pentagons, octagons, or four families held out from all development.

This project tested whether that pattern is predictable. The two-factor hypothesis — hardening helps where (1) the polygon differs substantially from its inscribed disk and (2) the best-known packings contain tilted orientations a rigid search cannot easily reach — did not survive. The flagship prediction was that concave pieces would show hardening's largest advantage; instead the pre-registered meta-analysis over 37 instances found no working predictor: not concavity (one-sided Fisher p=0.19), not a trend with piece count (Cochran–Armitage p=0.31), not inscribed-disk share (Mann–Whitney p=0.15). Hardening wins are real (9 of 37 instances, including replicated n=11 signatures and the L-tromino pinwheel), and the mechanism experiments show its role is basin delivery — a soft compress phase arranges positions, a short morph grows the corners in — but where it pays is not predicted by any shape descriptor tested. That negative result is the finding.

Concave pieces are compound: convex parts sharing one pose, with part-pair separating-axis collision and the morph applied to every vertex about the centroid. No Friedman-style catalogue exists for concave packings, so every concave claim is a relative head-to-head comparison at matched budget and frozen settings — never a record claim.

Status. 20 campaign(s), 73 instances, 5200 runs. Latest finding: C17: frozen-measure rule REPLICATES on second held-out batch (rho=-0.764, one-sided p=0.0137; bar was rho<-0.3, p<0.05). 6/8 wins. Cross wins twice more: n=14 squ (+0.036, fid 0.910) and n=11 tri (+0.219, fid 0.924) - both high-fidelity as predicted. Pattern: the fidelity CORRELATION is robust across 67 instances, but single-instance predictions are probabilistic (n=12 didn't replicate, n=14 did). Rule predicts the distribution, not individual flips.

Methods

  • harden — the search under study: γ = 1, τ : 1 → 0. Every piece starts as its inscribed disk (or centroid disk, for concave pieces) and hardens into the target shape while the container is pushed inward.
  • rigid — γ = 1, τ = 0 throughout: full pieces from the start, on the same schedule as hardening.
  • sa — rigid pieces, Metropolis simulated annealing on poses and container size.
  • pc — rigid pieces, perturbation–compression: shrink the container, relax overlaps, perturb on failure.

Budgets are matched across methods; every campaign is pre-registered with its decision rule before it runs, and results are append-only.
